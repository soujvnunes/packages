# Palette

A class names what a colour does, never what it is. Eight roles carry every colour of a screen, and the theme gives each role its value, so a rebrand is a change of values in one file.

## Roles

- `main`: a call to action, and high-emphasis text such as a display, headline, heading or title.
- `accent`: the companion of a call to action, a less important alternative beside it.
- `success`: a done or accepted state.
- `warning`: a state that needs attention before it becomes an error.
- `error`: a failed or refused state.
- `background`: the app ground only.
- `surface`: a card, a panel, a sheet, anything that sits on the ground.
- `neutral`: running text and a dismissible action. On an element over `main` or `accent` it behaves as them.

## Every role on every surface

The matrix has one row per ambient surface (light, dim, dark and high contrast) and one column per role. Each chip is the role's colour on that surface. The three dots inside a chip are text in the disabled, default and focused state, so a reader sees every role against every surface with its type states at once.

<ColourMatrix />

On a chip of `main`, `accent`, `success`, `warning` or `error`, text takes `neutral`, and the theme sets `neutral` to a dark ink there in every mode. On a `neutral` chip the dots take the `surface` colour, since `neutral` text on `neutral` would vanish.
