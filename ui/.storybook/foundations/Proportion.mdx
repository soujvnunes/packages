# Proportion

The spacing and sizing values form one ladder of steps. A container's proportions follow from its height: its inline padding is one step below its height, and its content (the font size, the icon) two steps below.

## Radius is concentric

An outer radius equals the inner radius plus the padding between them, so the two curves stay parallel. In the example, the inner box has a radius of 8 and the padding is 16, so the outer radius is 24.

<div className="foundations-panel sb-unstyled">
  <div className="foundations-concentric">
    <div data-tone="main">8 inside 16 makes 24</div>
  </div>
</div>

Three cases follow from it:

- A nested child takes the parent's inner radius.
- A close child attached to the parent's edge shares the parent's own radius on the touching side.
- An outside sibling keeps the default of its height.

A sum off the ladder means the padding is wrong: an inner 12 with a padding of 16 gives 28, which is no step, so move the padding to 12 or the inner radius to 8.

## Nested radius in the components

The components apply the rule themselves. Every control is a pill by default, and so are a Field and a ButtonGroup; a Bar takes no radius, since it spans what holds it, and a Card takes 24. A container publishes its inner radius, its own radius less its inline padding and never below zero, in a custom property named by its level: `--ui-inner-radius-64` for a Bar, a Field and a ButtonGroup, `--ui-inner-radius-card` for a Card, and `--ui-inner-radius-40` for a Button and an Input. Only a control with a numeric radius reads the property of what holds it before its own: a card button in a Card of radius 24 with 16 of inline padding takes 8, while the pill button beside it, and the pill input in a Field, stay pills. The first and last buttons of a ButtonGroup keep their pill on their outer side, and a Badge on a corner inherits the radius of that corner. A full radius stays a pill at every level.
